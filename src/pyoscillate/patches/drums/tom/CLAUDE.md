# Tom

## Sonic function

A tom is a pitched percussion voice: a short, resonant body with a distinct low-mid pitch and a quick downward sweep. It sits between a kick and a tonal melodic hit, giving the drum kit a pitched contour without requiring the full weight of a kick.

## Minimal architecture

trigger
→ pitched oscillator or resonant body
→ pitch envelope
→ amplitude envelope
→ optional transient or extra harmonic layer

## Pitch and body

The tom's character comes from the combination of:

- a higher fundamental than a kick
- a faster but still audible downward pitch sweep
- a resonant low-mid tonal body
- a short attack and medium decay

Use a simple oscillator or a resonant body to establish the main pitch. A gentle downward sweep gives the hit impact and separation, while the body carries the perceived pitch.

## Envelope behavior

Toms usually favor:

- short attack
- medium decay
- clear pitch movement
- smooth but audible resonance

Too little resonance makes the tom feel thin or synthetic; too much pitch movement makes it read like a zap or a pitched noise burst.

## Design alternatives

Low tom:
    deeper pitched body + shorter decay + soft sweep

Medium tom:
    clear pitch + moderate resonance + balanced tail

Bright tom:
    reduced body + more upper harmonic content + shorter, drier tail

## What to distinguish from

A tom should not be collapsed into:

- kick: lower, heavier, more impact-oriented
- snare: greater noise/rattle and more brittle transient
- bell: more overt partials and a longer, brighter decay

The primary identity is a pitched drum hit with a resonant body and a controllable sweep.
