# Kick

## Sonic function

A kick provides a short low-frequency impact and establishes
the rhythmic foundation. Its perceived identity comes primarily
from the relationship between attack, pitch movement, body,
and decay.

## Minimal architecture

trigger
→ pitch trajectory
→ tonal oscillator
→ amplitude envelope

Optional:
→ transient/noise layer
→ nonlinear saturation
→ filtering

The classic drum-voice version is intentionally simple: a sine oscillator is
started by a trigger, its pitch falls quickly, and a decaying amplitude
envelope shapes the body. The transient and saturation layers are optional
ways to adapt that core to a particular mix or style.

## Body

Sine oscillator is a common starting point because it provides
a strong fundamental with minimal harmonic content.

Perceptual:
- clean
- deep
- focused

Adding harmonics can increase:
- presence
- aggression
- audibility
- perceived density

## Pitch envelope

A rapid downward pitch movement at the beginning of the note
creates the characteristic pitch gesture of many electronic kicks.

Increasing:
- initial pitch → sharper / more pronounced attack
- sweep depth → stronger pitch gesture
- sweep duration → longer / more audible pitch movement

Too much:
- pitched sweep becomes obvious
- kick may resemble a tom or zap

## Amplitude envelope

Controls the temporal body.

Short decay:
- tight
- dry
- rhythmic
- leaves space

Long decay:
- sustained
- weighty
- boomy
- potentially masks bass

For a conventional electronic kick, the attack is zero because the impact
starts at maximum amplitude. A moderate decay with an exponential slope gives
a compact body; increasing the decay moves toward a booming 808-style result.

## Transient

A short noise or high-frequency component can clarify the attack.

More:
- click
- snap
- definition

Less:
- round
- soft
- integrated

## Saturation

Nonlinear processing adds harmonics.

Useful for:
- presence
- density
- aggression
- perceived loudness

Not fundamental to all kicks.

## Design alternatives

Clean house kick:
    sine + pitch envelope + amp envelope

Punchy electronic kick:
    sine + pitch envelope + transient

Aggressive techno kick:
    tonal body + pitch envelope + saturation

808-style:
    sine + relatively long amplitude decay +
    pronounced pitch envelope

## What NOT to assume

There is no universal:
- oscillator frequency
- pitch sweep amount
- decay time
- click duration
- distortion amount

The useful starting point is a relationship, not a preset: low sine body,
fast downward pitch movement, immediate attack, and a decay chosen around the
bassline. The kick should be judged in context with the rest of the groove.

These are style- and context-dependent.
